Redox Signaling Molecules

Your body produces molecules called Redox Signaling Molecules. You've been making them since before you were born. Every cell in your body depends on them.

They do two essential things:

They Protect

When a cell is damaged or under threat, redox signaling molecules activate your body's natural defense systems — telling the right cells to respond, repair, or replace what's been compromised.

They Communicate

They carry messages between cells — coordinating the countless processes that keep your body functioning. Healing a cut. Fighting off an illness. Recovering from a hard workout. Adapting to stress.

Think of them as the communication network your cells run on.

When you're young, your body produces these molecules abundantly. Communication is strong. Recovery is fast. Energy is high.

But production naturally declines over time:

~1% per year

Starting from puberty, your body produces roughly 1% fewer redox signaling molecules each year. Slowly, steadily, the network weakens.

The cells are still there. The design is still brilliant. But the messages aren't getting through the way they used to.

It's not that the body forgot what to do. It's that the body can't hear itself as clearly anymore.

How It Actually Works

Your Body Has a Thermostat

The orchestra analogy explains what redox signaling molecules do — they coordinate. But Dr. Lee Ostler, a practicing dentist and cellular health researcher, offers another analogy that explains how they do it.

Think of a thermostat on your wall.

It senses the conditions in the room. When something is off — too hot, too cold — it activates a response. The heating or cooling system kicks in, corrects the problem, and when everything is back in balance, the thermostat turns the system off.

Your cells work the same way.

Redox signaling molecules are your body's thermostat. They sense when something is wrong at the cellular level. They activate the gene pathways that respond to the problem — turning on your body's natural defense, repair, and antioxidant systems. And when the problem is corrected and balance is restored, they turn those systems off.

This is why redox signaling isn't just another supplement doing one thing. It's the regulatory mechanism behind everything else.
