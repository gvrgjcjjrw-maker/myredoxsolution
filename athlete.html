The Science

Redox Signaling Molecules

Your body produces molecules called Redox Signaling Molecules. You've been making them since before you were born. Every cell in your body depends on them.

They do two essential things:

They Protect

When cells are damaged — from training, stress, or environmental factors — redox signaling molecules activate your body's natural defense and repair systems, telling the right cells to respond.

They Communicate

They carry messages between cells, coordinating the processes that keep your body functioning at its peak — recovery, adaptation, immune response, and cellular renewal.

When the body is young and healthy, these molecules are abundant. Communication is strong. Recovery is fast. Adaptation happens quickly.

But production naturally declines over time:

~1% per year

Starting from puberty, your body produces roughly 1% fewer redox signaling molecules each year. Slowly, steadily, the network weakens.

The cells are still there. The design is still brilliant. But the messages aren't getting through the way they used to.

Your body doesn't forget how to perform. It just can't coordinate as efficiently as it once could.
