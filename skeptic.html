Five Minutes That Changed My Mind

It wasn't the presentation that changed my mind. It was the questions it made me ask.

The business model always made sense to me.

What never made sense was the idea that one company could have:

  • A truly unique product with no competition.
  • Fortune 50-caliber leadership grounded in trust and integrity.
  • Work that pays in ways you don't expect.

Frankly…

I didn't believe the magic-lamp fable — or its three wishes.

Then one day, my financial advisor asked if my wife and I would give a doctor colleague of his five minutes.

My wife was ready to head home. I said, "It's only five minutes." She agreed.

I listened. It sounded interesting enough that, on a whim, I bought the product.

I spent my career in corporate finance. My job was asking questions.

When it held my attention longer than anything else had, I started asking the only question that mattered.

What's the catch?

That question took me down a path I never expected. I went looking for a reason to walk away.

First, the company. Please, let the leadership be incompetent.

Instead, I discovered the founder had turned down an enormous pharmaceutical buyout — because the technology would have been shelved instead of brought to market. Damn.

Next, the person who introduced me. Please, let him have an angle.

He didn't. That would have made this whole thing a lot easier.

Then the product. Please, let it be another "me too" bottle.

Instead, I found something I couldn't compare to anything else I'd seen. Years of research. A wall of patents. Not something you slap a new label on and copy.

Then the question I'd saved for last. Is this just another network marketing company?

Yes. It is. I'm not going to hide that or dress it up.

But I realized I'd been asking the wrong first question. I wasn't deciding whether I wanted to build a business. I was trying to answer a much simpler one: Is the product real?

My favorite objection was also my last.

"It's just expensive salt water."

Honestly, I loved that one — because on the surface, it sounds completely reasonable.

But then I started reading. Independent researchers. Published studies. Scientists explaining their own work.

The deeper I looked, the harder it became to dismiss.

Don't take my word for it. Take theirs.

The study was published in the peer-reviewed literature: Shanely et al., "Influence of a redox-signaling supplement on biomarkers of physiological stress in athletes: a metabolomics approach," The FASEB Journal, 2012.

By this point, something strange was happening. I wasn't excited. I was frustrated.

Because every objection I crossed off left me with one less reason to walk away.

The decision to keep taking the product had already been made. That wasn't the difficult decision.

The difficult decision was whether I was willing to attach my reputation to it. That's a much higher bar.

After weeks of asking the same question —

What's the catch?

— I finally found myself asking a different one.

If this is real… it changes everything.

So here's the only thing I'll ask you to do.

Don't believe me. Don't take my word for it.

Do what I did. Ask questions. Read. Investigate. Challenge every claim.

Go looking for the catch yourself.

If you find one, you'll have your answer. If you don't… you may find yourself asking the same question I did.

If this is real… it changes everything.
